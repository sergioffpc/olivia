> Once upon a time there was **an astronaut cat** who had to save **the robot grandmother** from **the stinky socks** on **the ice cream planet**, picking up **cheeses** along the way!

The launcher is a fish cannon, and what it fires is a fish (`hero/launcher`, `fx/projectile`).

## Guide for the adult

**Before the class**

- Project page 1 or print it at A3; have markers and a visible timer (5 min).
- Test 2 or 3 complete stories at home with test drawings, including extreme cases (a huge and a tiny enemy, a dark background, a hero with thin arms).
- The gameplay (gravity, jumps, how enemies move) stays fixed: the class changes only drawings and sounds.

**During (about 1 minute per question)**

- Read the question, accept 2 or 3 ideas and write them on lines A, B, C. Don't debate ideas.
- Vote by show of hands; note the number and write the winner under "Winner".
- A tie? Combine the two ("an old-man astronaut!") or toss a coin.
- At the end, fill in page 2 and read the sentence aloud with the class.

**The only rules to defend**

- Hero and who is saved: 2 arms and 2 legs (because of the puppet). Present it as a rule of the game.
- Enemies: any shape or creature; their movement is already defined in the game.
- The world is always background + platforms + final object. The look changes, not the rules.

**If something goes off the rails**

- An impossible idea ("the hero is a ball")? Accept it as an enemy or as something to pick up.
- Nobody suggests anything? Give two options of your own and let them vote.
- Running out of time? Choose the missing answers yourself, based on the ideas already given.
